Spider-Man: Brand New Day

By Ross • August 2026

Year: 2026 • Genre: Action/Adventure

Rating: 6.5/10

A fun and emotional Spider-Man adventure with great performances and exciting action, but questionable character choices and missed opportunities prevent it from becoming one of the web-slinger's best films.

Review

Date Watched: August 4, 2026
Where Watched: Theaters
Adaptation? Marvel Comics

Quick Hook (First Impression)

Spider-Man holds a special place in my heart. He has been my favorite superhero ever since I was a kid, and I still remember seeing the original 2002 film in theaters. I recently even found my old Spider-Man and Green Goblin bobbleheads that I got for Christmas years ago, which brought back a lot of memories. More than that, I have always believed the Spider-Man films are among the greatest superhero movie collections ever made, so Spider-Man: Brand New Day was easily one of my most anticipated movies of the year. While I enjoyed the film and had a fun time watching it, I unfortunately didn't fall in love with it the way I had hoped.

Story / Concept

The story follows the aftermath of Spider-Man: No Way Home, where Peter Parker now lives in a world where everyone has forgotten he exists. Rather than rebuilding his personal life, Peter becomes completely consumed by his Spider-Man identity until it becomes the only life he knows.

Things become even more dangerous when Peter begins developing new mutations caused by his arachnid DNA slowly overtaking his human DNA. With the help of an inhibitor, Peter desperately tries to suppress the changes before eventually realizing that the mutation isn't something to fear. Instead, it represents his personal growth and his acceptance that Peter Parker and Spider-Man are not separate people—they are both part of who he truly is. I thought this was an interesting direction for Peter's character and one of the stronger aspects of the movie.

Performances

Tom Holland once again delivers a strong performance as Peter Parker, continuing to balance the humor, emotion, and vulnerability that have made his version of Spider-Man so successful. Zendaya also gives another solid performance as MJ, and the emotional scenes between the two remain some of the film's strongest moments.

The biggest surprise was Sadie Sink as Jean Grey. She brings confidence, emotion, and mystery to the role while giving audiences their first real taste of the upcoming Mutant Saga in the Marvel Cinematic Universe. Even with limited screen time, she leaves a lasting impression and makes me excited to see where Marvel takes the X-Men next.

Pacing / Flow

Despite its lengthy two-hour-and-twenty-five-minute runtime, the film moves at a steady pace and rarely feels slow. The action is spread throughout the movie, keeping things entertaining while allowing the emotional moments room to breathe. My favorite sequence is Spider-Man battling The Hand inside the Department of Damage Control prison. The choreography is creative, energetic, and reminds audiences just how fun Spider-Man's fighting style can be when fully utilized.

While the pacing itself is solid, I do think several supporting characters and villains deserved more screen time. Tombstone, in particular, feels like a missed opportunity. Rather than becoming a significant threat, he's reduced to little more than a brief appearance during the opening montage. As one of Spider-Man's notable street-level villains, I couldn't help but feel he deserved a much larger role.

Themes / Meaning

At its core, Spider-Man: Brand New Day is about identity and acceptance. Peter has sacrificed so much that he slowly begins losing himself within the Spider-Man persona. The mutation storyline becomes a metaphor for accepting every part of who he is instead of trying to separate Peter Parker from Spider-Man. It's a meaningful message that continues Peter's emotional journey following the events of No Way Home.

The emotional scenes between Peter and MJ are among the film's strongest moments because you can genuinely feel Peter's internal conflict. He desperately wants to tell MJ who he is, but believes protecting her means continuing to hide the truth. That struggle pays off emotionally once MJ discovers the letter Peter wrote to her, creating one of the movie's most heartfelt scenes.

Final Thoughts

While Spider-Man: Brand New Day succeeds as an entertaining superhero film with strong performances, exciting action, and meaningful emotional moments, several creative decisions keep it from reaching the heights of Spider-Man's very best movies.

My biggest issue was the excessive profanity throughout the film. I understand Marvel has been pushing some of its recent films and Disney+ series toward slightly more mature storytelling, but the repeated use of strong language—particularly the S-word and "GD"—felt unnecessary and distracting.

That issue becomes especially noticeable with Jon Bernthal's Punisher. I love Bernthal's portrayal of Frank Castle, and he works perfectly in Daredevil and The Punisher, but he never truly feels at home in Spider-Man's PG-13 world. Unlike Wolverine transitioning into the R-rated Deadpool & Wolverine, where the character's edge naturally fit the tone, The Punisher feels watered down here, making some of his dialogue come across as awkward and out of place.

Another character that felt somewhat unnecessary was Bruce Banner. I understand his purpose in helping explain Peter's inhibitor, but the movie never properly explains why Bruce suddenly requires similar technology after previously becoming Smart Hulk in Avengers: Endgame and She-Hulk. Unless I missed something in another MCU project, this felt like an unnecessary continuity issue.

Overall, I do think Spider-Man: Brand New Day is a good movie. The performances are excellent, the action is entertaining, and the themes surrounding Peter's identity are compelling. However, as someone who has loved Spider-Man since childhood, this simply wasn't the Spider-Man movie I was hoping to get.

Who is this for? Spider-Man fans, MCU followers, and anyone looking for an entertaining superhero movie with strong emotional moments and exciting action.

Who should skip it? Anyone expecting another Spider-Man: No Way Home or those looking for one of Spider-Man's strongest cinematic adventures may come away disappointed.

One-Sentence Verdict: Spider-Man: Brand New Day delivers exciting action, heartfelt performances, and meaningful themes, but inconsistent character choices and missed opportunities keep it from becoming one of Spider-Man's greatest films.

What Worked

  • Excellent performances from Tom Holland, Zendaya, and Sadie Sink.
  • Strong emotional story centered around Peter's identity.
  • Exciting action sequences, especially the Department of Damage Control prison fight.
  • Jean Grey provides an exciting introduction to the MCU's Mutant Saga.

What Didn't

  • Excessive profanity throughout the film.
  • The Punisher feels out of place in a PG-13 Spider-Man movie.
  • Bruce Banner's role creates unnecessary continuity questions.
  • Tombstone and several other villains are underused.

Optional Gut Check

Rewatchable? Yes

Would I recommend this casually to a friend? Yes
