Apex (2026)

Genre: Action Thriller Runtime: 1 hr 35 min Date Watched: June 11, 2026 Where Watched: Netflix Adaptation: No

The ways we watch films have definitely changed, to say the least. Back then I remember going to the movies with my grandmother, finding the movie I wanted to watch, sitting through trailers for future releases, and then settling in for the feature presentation. Nowadays streaming services have, in some ways, taken the charm out of the experience and replaced it with convenience.

Don't get me wrong, I still enjoy watching films that premiere on streaming services. In fact, some of my favorite recent films such as The Harder They Fall, Mr. Crocket, War Machine, and The Irishman all came from streaming platforms. Because of that, I'm always looking for the next hidden gem.

While browsing Netflix, I came across Apex and decided to give it a chance. After watching it, I can honestly say it's one of those middle-of-the-pack films that has some bright spots but never fully capitalizes on its potential.

The story follows Sasha (Charlize Theron), an adrenaline junkie who is grieving the loss of her lover (Eric Bana) while exploring the Australian wilderness. During one final thrill-seeking adventure, she encounters Ben (Taron Egerton), a seemingly friendly explorer who turns out to be a psychopathic hunter. What follows is a cat-and-mouse game where Sasha must fight for survival.

While the premise sounds interesting, the film ultimately falls into the category of "been there, done that." Survival thrillers like this have been made many times before, and Apex never does enough to separate itself from the pack. The story hits most of the expected beats without offering much in the way of surprises outside of one major twist.

Charlize Theron's performance was one that I had a difficult time investing in. For much of the film she looks and sounds like she's simply going through the motions. Despite Sasha's tragic backstory, there isn't much emotional weight behind the performance. There is a nice monologue later in the film that helps flesh out the character, but outside of that scene her portrayal feels surprisingly dry.

Thankfully, Taron Egerton more than makes up for it with his portrayal of Ben. He was easily the MVP of the movie for me. At first Ben appears to be a friendly and helpful explorer, but as the film progresses his true colors begin to emerge. Once he fully embraces his darker side, Egerton turns in a genuinely menacing performance that elevates nearly every scene he's in.

I also found the film's biggest twist involving Ben and his beef jerky to be genuinely shocking. It was one of the few moments where the story completely caught me off guard and provided the kind of tension I was hoping for throughout the rest of the movie.

The biggest positive I can give Apex is its cinematography. The camera work is especially impressive during the kayak sequences, where the transitions from above the water to underwater create some truly beautiful visuals. Director Baltasar Kormákur does an excellent job showcasing the Australian landscape through stunning wide shots that make the setting feel alive and immersive.

Unfortunately, I only wish the story was as beautiful as the scenery. The opening act takes a while to get going, and the ending ultimately falls flat. After spending so much time building tension, the final payoff feels surprisingly anticlimactic.

In the end, Apex has enough positives to make it watchable, particularly thanks to Taron Egerton's performance and the film's gorgeous cinematography. However, its familiar story, underwhelming lead performance, and weak ending keep it from being anything more than an average thriller.

What Worked

  • Taron Egerton delivers the film's standout performance.
  • Beautiful cinematography throughout the Australian wilderness.
  • Excellent kayak sequences and underwater camera work.
  • A shocking twist that adds much-needed tension.

What Didn't

  • The story feels overly familiar.
  • Charlize Theron's performance lacks emotional investment.
  • The opening act is slow.
  • The ending feels anticlimactic.

Final Verdict

Who is this for?
Fans of survival thrillers, wilderness adventures, and cat-and-mouse suspense stories.

Who should skip it?
Viewers looking for a fresh take on the genre or expecting one of Charlize Theron's best performances.

Rewatchable? Maybe

Would I recommend this casually to a friend? No

One-Sentence Verdict:
Apex boasts beautiful visuals and a fantastic villain performance from Taron Egerton, but its familiar story and weak payoff prevent it from reaching greater heights.

Rating: 5.5/10
