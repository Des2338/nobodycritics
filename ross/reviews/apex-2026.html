Apex (2026)

Genre: Action Thriller Runtime: 1 hr 35 min Date Watched: June 11, 2026 Where Watched: Netflix Adaptation: No

The way we watch movies has changed dramatically over the years. I remember going to the theater with my grandmother, picking out a movie, sitting through trailers for upcoming releases, and then settling in for the feature presentation. Streaming services have largely replaced that experience with convenience. While some of the magic has been lost, streaming has also delivered some excellent films. Movies like The Harder They Fall, Mr. Crocket, War Machine, and The Irishman proved that great films can come from these platforms, so I'm always looking for the next hidden gem.

While browsing Netflix, I came across Apex and decided to give it a shot. After watching it, I can confidently say it's a middle-of-the-road thriller that has some bright spots but never fully reaches its potential.

The story follows Sasha (Charlize Theron), an adrenaline junkie grieving the loss of her lover (Eric Bana) while exploring the Australian wilderness. During one final thrill-seeking adventure, she crosses paths with Ben (Taron Egerton), a seemingly friendly explorer who turns out to be a psychopathic hunter. What follows is a cat-and-mouse game for survival through the Australian outback.

On paper, the premise sounds interesting enough, but the film struggles to bring anything new to the familiar survival-thriller formula. Much of the story feels like territory that has already been covered by better films, and the script never does enough to distinguish itself from its influences.

Charlize Theron's performance was surprisingly difficult for me to connect with. For most of the film, she appears to be going through the motions, delivering a performance that feels emotionally distant despite the character's tragic backstory. There is one particularly effective monologue later in the film that gives her some depth, but outside of that moment, her portrayal feels somewhat dry.

Fortunately, Taron Egerton picks up the slack and delivers the film's standout performance. Ben is easily the most interesting character in the movie. Egerton does an excellent job slowly revealing the character's darker side before fully unleashing his psychotic tendencies. Once Ben's true nature emerges, he becomes a genuinely intimidating presence and the film immediately becomes more engaging. A major twist involving Ben and his beef jerky was genuinely shocking and ended up being one of the few story developments that completely caught me off guard.

The film's strongest technical aspect is its cinematography. The kayak sequences are particularly impressive, with smooth transitions between above-water and underwater shots that create some memorable visuals. Director Baltasar Kormákur makes excellent use of the Australian landscape, filling the film with beautiful wide shots that showcase the wilderness and help establish a strong sense of place.

Unfortunately, the story isn't nearly as compelling as the scenery. The opening act takes too long to get going, and the ending ultimately feels anticlimactic after all the buildup. By the time the credits rolled, I was left feeling that the film had squandered an interesting setup.

In the end, Apex has enough positives to make it watchable, particularly thanks to Taron Egerton's performance and the gorgeous cinematography. However, a familiar story, an underwhelming lead performance, and a weak conclusion prevent it from rising above average.

What Worked

  • Taron Egerton delivers a memorable and intimidating performance.
  • Beautiful cinematography, especially during the kayak sequences.
  • The twist involving Ben was genuinely surprising.
  • Strong use of the Australian wilderness setting.

What Didn't

  • Story feels overly familiar and doesn't add much to the survival-thriller genre.
  • Charlize Theron's performance lacks emotional investment.
  • The ending feels anticlimactic.
  • Slow opening and uneven pacing.

Final Verdict

Who Is This For?
Fans of survival thrillers, wilderness-set suspense films, and viewers who enjoy cat-and-mouse stories.

Who Should Skip It?
Anyone looking for a fresh take on the genre or expecting one of Charlize Theron's stronger performances.

Rewatchable? Maybe

Would I Recommend It to a Friend? No

One-Sentence Verdict:
Apex offers strong visuals and a great villain performance, but its familiar story and underwhelming execution keep it from reaching greater heights.

Rating: 5.5/10
