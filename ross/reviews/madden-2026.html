Madden

By Ross • November 2026

Year: 2026 • Genre: Drama / Comedy

Rating: 2/10

Madden tells the story of one of the most iconic figures in football history, but unfortunately, this biopic is a massive disappointment. Between exaggerated performances, a lack of chemistry, and rushed dramatic moments, the movie wastes an opportunity to tell the story of a sports legend.

Review

Date Watched: November 23, 2026
Where Watched: Prime Video
Adaptation? No

Quick Hook (First Impression)

John Madden is one of the most recognizable and iconic figures in football history. From his days coaching the Raiders, where he never had a losing record throughout his coaching career, to his legendary broadcasting career and eventually having one of the most successful sports video game franchises named after him, Madden accomplished just about everything imaginable to help turn the NFL into the sporting juggernaut it is today.

His influence on the sport extends far beyond the football field, making him one of the most important personalities in the history of the game. With a legacy like that, it only makes sense to have a biopic showing audiences how it all came to be.

As a sports fan myself, I was very excited to see this film, particularly with the casting of Nicolas Cage as the titular character, John Madden, and Christian Bale as Raiders owner Al Davis. With two recognizable actors taking on the roles of two larger-than-life personalities, I thought there was plenty of potential for an entertaining sports biopic.

Unfortunately, after watching the film, all I can say is that my disappointment is an understatement. What should have been an interesting look into the life and career of a football legend instead turns into a frustrating experience that struggles to find its identity. This could easily be one of the worst films I've seen this year.

Story / Concept

The biggest disappointment with Madden is that the film has such an interesting subject to work with but never manages to make the most of it. John Madden's life and career provide plenty of material for a compelling biopic. His success as a coach, his relationship with the Raiders organization, his transition into broadcasting, and the eventual creation of the Madden video game franchise are all fascinating parts of his legacy. There is a story worth telling here, but the movie never quite figures out how to tell it in an engaging way.

One of the central storylines revolves around Al Davis trying to convince John to return to coaching, despite the wishes of John's wife, Virginia, played by Kathryn Hahn. On paper, this could have provided an interesting conflict between John's professional ambitions, his personal life, and his relationship with the Raiders organization.

It also could have explored the complicated relationship between two influential football personalities who helped shape the sport in their own ways. Unfortunately, the film never gives this conflict the depth it needs to become particularly engaging.

Instead, the story jumps between different events and conflicts without allowing much time for any of them to develop. There are several potentially interesting directions the movie could have taken, especially when exploring what motivated Madden throughout his career and how his growing success affected his family.

However, these ideas are introduced without being explored in any meaningful way. It feels like the filmmakers were more interested in getting through the major events of Madden's life than actually telling a compelling story about the man behind the legend.

Performances

One of the biggest problems with the film is its performances, with the biggest disappointments being Nicolas Cage and Christian Bale as John Madden and Raiders owner Al Davis, respectively. Both performances are so exaggerated and over the top that it feels like I'm watching an SNL skit that somehow goes on for nearly two hours.

There is a difference between portraying larger-than-life personalities and turning those personalities into caricatures, and unfortunately, this movie struggles to understand that distinction.

Now, having exaggerated performances wouldn't necessarily be a problem if the actors had any kind of chemistry, but unfortunately, that is nowhere to be found. The relationship between Madden and Davis should be one of the most interesting aspects of the movie. These are two very recognizable figures in football history, and their interactions should provide some of the film's most entertaining and dramatic moments.

Instead, their scenes rarely feel natural, and their interactions lack the energy needed to make their relationship convincing.

This becomes especially noticeable during the film's more dramatic moments. As mentioned earlier, one of the conflicts involves Al trying to persuade John to return to coaching against Virginia's wishes.

However, the emotional side of this storyline never really comes together. We get moments such as John realizing that he has missed his sons growing up, the stomach ulcer that contributed to his retirement, and Virginia leaving John before eventually returning after Al's wife falls into a coma.

These are significant moments that should provide some emotional weight to the story, particularly when showing the sacrifices that come with dedicating so much of your life to a career.

Unfortunately, the performances never quite sell these moments. Rather than feeling invested in what the characters are going through, I found myself watching scenes that felt more like actors going through the motions. It is especially frustrating because these moments could have given the movie some much-needed emotional depth.

A story about someone as successful as John Madden should also be willing to explore the personal sacrifices and difficulties that came with that success. Instead, the film touches on these subjects without giving them the attention they deserve.

Pacing / Flow

The pacing is another major issue because the movie never seems to settle into a consistent rhythm. At times, it feels like the filmmakers are rushing to get from one major event to another without considering whether the audience has had enough time to process what is happening.

Whenever a scene begins introducing a conflict, the movie quickly rushes through the dramatic problem before jumping toward an unresolved solution, only to repeat the same process with the next conflict.

This becomes particularly frustrating when the film attempts to deliver emotional scenes. Moments involving John's family, his retirement, and his relationship with Al should have been given more room to develop. Instead, they come and go so quickly that they never have the opportunity to leave much of an impression.

It is difficult to become emotionally invested in a character's struggles when the movie barely gives those struggles enough time to exist.

The pacing also hurts the overall storytelling because there isn't enough breathing room between the comedic and dramatic moments. The movie constantly shifts between trying to be a comedy and attempting to deliver serious character drama, but neither side feels particularly well-developed.

Rather than creating a natural balance between the two, the transitions make the film feel disjointed. At 1 hour and 50 minutes, the runtime isn't particularly long, but the problem isn't necessarily that the movie needed to be significantly longer. It needed to use the time it had more effectively.

Themes / Meaning

Perhaps the most frustrating aspect of Madden is the missed opportunity to explore what made John Madden such an important figure. His legacy is about much more than coaching football. He became a recognizable voice in sports broadcasting and helped introduce generations of fans to football through his video game franchise.

His impact reached people who might never have watched him coach a game, which is a testament to just how influential he became.

A biopic about someone with that kind of legacy should leave the audience with a greater appreciation for the person and the impact they had on their industry. Unfortunately, I never felt that the movie accomplished this.

It touches on different aspects of Madden's life, but it doesn't do enough to connect those experiences into a meaningful story. There is also an interesting personal side to explore, particularly the sacrifices involved in balancing a demanding career with family life, but the film never gives those themes the emotional attention they need.

Ultimately, the movie feels more concerned with presenting recognizable moments from Madden's life than explaining why those moments mattered. That is a major problem when your subject is someone whose influence is still felt throughout professional football and sports entertainment today.

Final Thoughts

Madden is a frustratingly disappointing biopic that wastes the story of one of football's biggest legends with exaggerated performances, poor pacing, and a lack of emotional depth.

If I had to point out some positives, I will say that I enjoyed a couple of the jokes throughout the film. One particular moment that got a laugh out of me involved Al Davis' son, Mark, coming downstairs and one of the EA developers making a comment about him being Al's child because of his trademark bowl haircut. It was a pretty funny moment that stood out among the movie's otherwise disappointing attempts at comedy.

I also enjoyed the sequences where John Madden walked the audience through some of the plays from his playbook, with the film showing how those specific plays would work on the football field.

These moments were easily among the more interesting parts of the movie because they gave audiences an opportunity to see Madden's football knowledge in action. They also served as a reminder of how much thought and strategy went into his approach to the game. I would have liked to see more of these sequences because they provided something genuinely entertaining and informative.

Unfortunately, those few moments aren't nearly enough to save the film. Outside of a couple of decent jokes and some interesting football sequences, there really isn't much positive I can say about Madden. The performances are exaggerated, the chemistry between the leads is practically nonexistent, and the pacing prevents the more dramatic moments from having any real impact.

It is especially disappointing because the subject matter had so much potential. John Madden's life is the kind of story that should practically write itself, yet the film somehow struggles to make it interesting.

Who is this for? Football fans who are interested in John Madden's life and legacy may find a few entertaining moments, particularly the sequences involving his football strategies and influence on the game.

Who should skip it? Anyone expecting a well-crafted biopic with strong performances, meaningful character development, and an emotional story. Even fans of John Madden may find themselves disappointed by how his story is handled.

One-Sentence Verdict: Madden is a frustratingly disappointing biopic that wastes the story of one of football's biggest legends with exaggerated performances, poor pacing, and a lack of emotional depth.

What Worked

  • A couple of genuinely funny jokes, particularly the joke involving Mark Davis and his trademark bowl haircut.
  • The sequences showing John Madden explaining plays from his playbook and demonstrating how they worked on the field.
  • The opportunity to learn more about the football knowledge and strategies that helped make Madden such an influential figure.

What Didn't

  • Nicolas Cage and Christian Bale deliver exaggerated performances that lack chemistry.
  • The dramatic moments involving John's family, retirement, and relationship with Al Davis lack emotional impact.
  • Poor pacing prevents the story from developing naturally.
  • The movie struggles to balance its comedy and drama.
  • A massive missed opportunity to tell the story of an iconic sports figure.
