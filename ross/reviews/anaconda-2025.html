Anaconda

By Ross • 2025

Year: 2025 • Genre: Comedy • Runtime: TBD

Rating: 5/10

A comedy-first meta reboot that lands laughs but completely forgets to be scary.

Review

Anaconda (2025) is a not-horror comedy and the newest entry in the Anaconda franchise. When I first heard about the film, I was intrigued—I liked the first two movies but absolutely hated the direct-to-TV sequels. After the first trailer, my interest dropped since it looked shallow and dumb. After finally watching it, I can say the comedy works very well, but the horror is completely nonexistent.

The biggest strength of the film lies in its characters, particularly the core four: Jack Black, Paul Rudd, Steve Zahn, and Thandiwe Newton. They play longtime friends who love the original Anaconda and filmmaking in general. Their chemistry feels genuine, especially between Paul Rudd and Thandiwe Newton, whose characters share romantic tension. The standout performance is Steve Zahn as Kenny—a dimwitted but lovable presence who delivers the film’s funniest moments.

Another positive is that the story understands exactly what it’s trying to be. The movie is a meta take on the 1997 original, openly poking fun at itself, Hollywood’s reboot obsession, and the lack of originality in modern studio filmmaking. Cameos from Ice Cube and Jennifer Lopez in a post-credits scene are respectful nods that avoid leaning too hard on nostalgia.

Unfortunately, the weaknesses are hard to ignore. Despite being marketed as a horror comedy, there is almost no horror to speak of. Jump scares are predictable, rare, and ineffective. A rushed subplot involving gold-mining thieves and Daniela Melchior’s character, Ana, feels underdeveloped and ends before it can leave any impact.

Character arcs are also extremely predictable. Griffin (Rudd) is a struggling actor, Doug (Black) is a wedding videographer who insists on calling his work “films,” and Claire (Newton) is a divorcee reconnecting with Griffin. Their stories unfold exactly as expected, leaving Kenny as the only character who exists outside narrative obligation.

The biggest disappointment is the snake itself. While the concept isn’t bad, the execution is weak. Shaky camerawork, blink-and-you-miss-it edits, and overly dark lighting—especially during night scenes—rob the creature of any sense of terror. The kills are few and largely unmemorable.

Final Thoughts

Anaconda (2025) is an enjoyable watch if you’re in it for the comedy and the cast’s chemistry. However, horror fans—especially those who love creature features—will likely walk away disappointed. It’s funny, self-aware, and occasionally clever, but it never delivers on the horror side of its premise.

What Worked

  • Strong comedic chemistry among the core cast
  • Steve Zahn’s performance as Kenny
  • Self-aware, meta approach to reboot culture

What Didn’t

  • Almost no horror despite the marketing
  • Weak and rushed subplot involving the thieves
  • Poor presentation of the snake and its attacks
