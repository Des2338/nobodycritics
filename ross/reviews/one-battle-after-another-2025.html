One Battle After Another (2025)

Genre: Comedy, Drama, Action

Runtime: 2 hr 42 min

Director: Paul Thomas Anderson

Initial Thoughts

The Oscars have finally come and gone, and to say that 2025 was a successful year for movies would be an understatement. With films like Sinners, Marty Supreme, Weapons, and Zootopia 2 all making noise, the competition was stacked. However, the film that stood above them all—and took home Best Picture—was One Battle After Another.

Performances

Leonardo DiCaprio delivers a standout performance as Bob Ferguson, bringing emotional weight to the role of a father trying to protect his daughter while confronting his past. Benicio del Toro shines as Sergio St. Carlos, the calm and collected counterbalance to Bob’s chaos. Sean Penn is chilling as Steven J. Lockjaw, earning his Best Supporting Actor win with a performance that creates immediate tension, especially in scenes with Willa (Chase Infiniti).

Direction & Tone

Paul Thomas Anderson masterfully blends comedy, drama, action, and suspense, creating a film that never feels forced or overstuffed. The story flows naturally and the tonal balance enhances both character and narrative depth.

Story

While the premise—a father confronting his past to save his daughter—may seem familiar, the execution feels fresh and engaging. Strong character work and emotional stakes keep the audience invested throughout the nearly three-hour runtime.

Pros

  • Strong, layered performances
  • Excellent character dynamics, especially Bob vs. Sergio
  • Natural blending of multiple genres
  • Engaging, character-driven story
  • Deserved Best Picture winner

Cons

  • Pacing drags in parts of the long runtime
  • Bob’s backstory (French 75) could use more exploration

Who Is This For?

  • Fans of Leonardo DiCaprio and strong performances
  • Viewers who enjoy genre-blending films
  • People who like character-driven stories
  • Fans of Paul Thomas Anderson

Who Might Skip It?

  • Viewers who prefer fast-paced action
  • Those not into longer films
  • People who dislike slower, character-focused stories

Rewatchability

Yes – There’s a lot to notice with performances and character dynamics.

Would I Recommend It?

Yes – Strong acting, layered storytelling, and genre balance make it a must-watch.

Final Thoughts

Despite minor pacing issues, One Battle After Another succeeds in its characters, story, and performances. It’s both massive and personal, and fully deserves its Best Picture win.

Rating

9.5/10
