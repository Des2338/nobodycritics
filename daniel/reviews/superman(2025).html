Superman

By Daniel • December 2025

Year: 2025 • Genre: Superhero • Runtime: 2h 9m

Rating: 7.5/10

Superman (2025) doesn’t waste time retelling the origin story — and that’s a good thing. This is a Superman who’s already established and already loved. Visually, the film looks great, and it leans more into the source material than any other Superman movie.

Review

The opening text makes it clear we’re not watching the start of Superman again, which I appreciated because I’ve seen it enough. Instead, the film focuses on the early years of being the hero after he’s already stepped into the role — and already taken his first real ass-kicking about three minutes in.

We also get the dog — more like pup — and his lair is already set up and ready to run. The authentic feel hits immediately. The colors, sets, and costume design all feel natural, not forced or overdesigned. The DP and the colorist did their jobs and did them right.

One thing that felt off early on were the robots that handle the medical side and act almost like butlers. That felt out of place. It leaned more toward something you’d expect in a Batman movie than a Superman one. Superman’s healing is usually about time and soaking up sunlight. That idea is still there, but the way it’s presented felt more Batman-style to me.

Characters & Story

The story keeps Superman front and center as he tries to find balance in a world with too many opinions on right and wrong — not just as a hero, but morally and emotionally. He’s trying to find middle ground so people can coexist in a functioning society.

Lex Luthor, played by Nicholas Hoult, is one of the strongest parts of the film. There are moments where you can understand his logic. Superman is an alien, and from a human perspective, maybe he is a potential threat. That moral tension makes Lex more interesting, even before he crosses clear lines. He’s the perfect villain for Superman.

Guy Gardner (Nathan Fillion) was simply not the right Green Lantern for this story. His behavior actively undermines Superman rather than strengthening him. This isn’t the kind of support Superman needs — especially in a story drawing inspiration from All-Star Superman. He needed a Green Lantern who could stand beside him with clarity and restraint, not one who disrupts the tone with ego and humor.

Behind the Camera

The opening shot of Superman crashing into the snow was beautiful. The landscape shots, paired with warm sunrise yellows and cool blues wrapping around nearly 360 degrees, really stood out. Many shots throughout the film use strong leading lines, guiding your attention exactly where it needs to go. The composition stays solid from start to finish.

James Gunn mentioned leaning on handheld camera work. Some shots may be fully handheld, but it’s clear stabilization rigs were used extensively. That explains how the camera stays smooth throughout the film. Nothing felt disorienting or distracting, which is exactly what we want for a film like this.

One thing I didn’t realize until after watching the film is that Robot 4 — also known as Gary — wasn’t fully CGI. The production used a physical suit and a performer on set, giving the character real presence and timing. CGI was layered on to enhance facial details and mechanics. The emotion and restraint in Gary’s movement came from a human performer, not animation alone.

Lighting & Color

Lighting and color are some of the film’s strongest elements. Darker, more emotional moments lean into blues and muted tones. Scenes meant to inspire hope or highlight heroism use warmer, brighter colors.

There’s an early moment in Lex Luthor’s lair where the sunlight feels almost blinding. It borders on being overdone, but it feels intentional — a reminder that no matter how powerful Lex believes himself to be, Superman will always overshadow him.

A great example of how the film handles darker tones is the apartment scene between Superman and Lois. While the Justice Gang is fighting a monster in the background filled with vibrant color, the foreground stays grounded and restrained, letting the contrast do the work.

“Oh Sh*t” Moments (Spoiler-Light)

One big “oh sh*t” moment was finding out who Jimmy’s source really was. I didn’t see that coming. I assumed she was just a dumb young gold digger and didn’t pay her much attention. Turns out, she was smart — and she ended up being Lex Luthor’s Achilles’ heel. Looking back, I probably should have caught it, but I didn’t, and it genuinely surprised me.

Another major moment was when Lex straight-up shoots that guy. Lex playing Russian roulette to force Superman to talk was intense. He thought the game would work, but when the bullet fired, Lex was shocked and irritated because now he had to find another victim. That scene made it clear Lex had crossed the point of no return.

Themes & Meaning

The main theme running through the film is not trusting things at face value. Media is portrayed as a constant villain — quick to judge and careless about the damage it causes.

The film pushes ideas about treating people as humans first, not rushing to judgment, and trying to do the right thing. News feeds and social media are driven more by clicks and reactions than truth. If you didn’t see it yourself, media is the last place you should fully trust.

Verdict

Final Thoughts

Overall, I enjoyed Superman (2025). I didn’t go in with huge expectations, and I ended up enjoying my time in Metropolis. The cast across the board did a great job.

David Corenswet did good as Superman. A lot of people compared him to Henry Cavill, but this version feels more grounded in comic-book source material, and that worked for this universe.

Superman represents what we all want to be, but the problem is we let too many outside sources dictate how we treat one another. He’s uncorruptible, strong, caring, and always trying to do the right thing — and like all of us, it’s a struggle.

Nicholas Hoult as Lex Luthor was fantastic. In my opinion, he’s the best Lex Luthor we’ve gotten in a Superman film. He’s a real villain, but what makes him work is that you can sometimes see the logic behind what he’s saying — even when he clearly goes too far.

I also have to mention Isabella Merced as Hawkgirl — man, was she hot. There’s no way around it. You’ve got to admire how attractive she was on screen. Honestly, I’d love to see a standalone movie with her as Hawkgirl. Just saying, DC Studios — don’t change the casting.

As much as I tried not to view the film through a modern political lens, it was hard not to notice parallels to current issues around immigration and identity. Those moments feel intentional, but they never overpower the human message.

My favorite scene in the entire film is still the moment when Superman goes home to recover from kryptonite poisoning and talks with his dad outside. His father recognizing both his physical and emotional pain, and reminding him that a parent’s job is to help their child become who they’re meant to be, carried real emotional weight. As a parent, that scene resonated deeply with me.

My biggest issue remains the overuse of comedic relief. James Gunn’s Guardians of the Galaxy fingerprints are all over the film. Comedy hits too fast after serious moments and weakens emotional impact. Most of the humor should have stayed with the super dog — those moments always worked.

If you haven’t seen it, go watch it. Don’t let online chatter scare you away, and stop comparing it to Zack Snyder’s Superman. They’re different takes, and both can work.

What Worked

  • Colors and lighting
  • Strong cast performances
  • Lex Luthor as a real villain

What Didn’t

  • Too much comedic relief
  • Emotional moments that felt too juvenile
  • Green Lantern’s portrayal
